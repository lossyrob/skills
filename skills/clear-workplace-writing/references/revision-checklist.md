# Revision checklist

Revise in this order. Sentence polish is last because elegant sentences cannot rescue a bad information hierarchy.

## 1. Meaning and factual preservation

- Preserve every material fact, number, qualification, commitment, constraint, and open question from the source.
- Do not strengthen uncertainty without evidence.
- Do not add an implication merely because it sounds plausible.
- Keep technical distinctions that affect correctness or decisions.

## 2. Audience and purpose

- Can the intended reader tell what kind of document this is and what it asks of them?
- Does an engineering reader get enough mechanism and constraints to inspect the reasoning?
- Does a leadership reader get the conclusion, significance, evidence, tradeoff, risk, and ask without mining technical detail?
- For a mixed audience, does the document progressively disclose detail rather than choosing one audience and abandoning the other?

## 3. Structure

- Is the governing point early enough?
- Are supporting claims grouped under the idea they support?
- Is discovery chronology masquerading as an argument?
- Does every section earn its place?
- Do headings reveal useful structure?

## 4. Reasoning

- Are observation, inference, assumption, recommendation, and prediction distinguishable?
- Are causal claims connected by an explicit mechanism or evidence?
- Are alternatives compared against stated criteria?
- Are risks and uncertainty visible rather than softened into vague prose?

## 5. Paragraphs and sentences

- Does each paragraph have one controlling job?
- Do sentence openings connect to established context?
- Are important new facts placed where they receive emphasis?
- Are actors and actions clear?
- Are related words close enough that the reader does not have to reconstruct the sentence?

## 6. Words

Cut or replace:

- empty intensifiers and puffery;
- bureaucratic circumlocution;
- repeated conclusions;
- synonym rotation for the same concept;
- vague verbs when an exact action is known;
- abstract nouns that hide the actor or mechanism;
- generic transition phrases that add no logical relationship;
- canned openings and closings.

Do not cut:

- evidence needed to trust the conclusion;
- caveats that change the decision;
- technical terms that carry real precision;
- examples that remove ambiguity;
- deliberate rhythm or voice that helps the reader.

## 7. Final test

Ask:

> What work is the reader being forced to do that the writer could have done instead?

Fix that work when doing so preserves accuracy and useful nuance.
