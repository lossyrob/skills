# Source notes

This skill synthesizes durable pre-LLM writing guidance rather than treating any one guide as doctrine. The rules are original summaries and adaptations, not reproduced copyrighted text.

## Major influences

### Herbert Spencer, *The Philosophy of Style* (1852)

Primary contribution: minimize unnecessary mental effort for the reader. This skill turns that idea into its governing objective: optimize for reader effort rather than word count alone.

### Edwin A. Abbott, *How to Write Clearly* (19th century)

Primary contribution: clarity can be improved through repeatable attention to ambiguity, word placement, and the relation between sentence parts.

### H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler, *The King's English* (1906)

Primary contribution: precision of usage, avoidance of circumlocution and ambiguity, and skepticism toward ornamental variation.

### William Strunk Jr., *The Elements of Style* (1918)

Primary contribution: concrete language, economy, paragraph discipline, proximity of related words, active constructions when useful, and emphasis through placement.

This skill deliberately weakens several Strunk rules from absolutes into reader-centered heuristics. Active voice is useful because agency often matters, not because passive voice is inherently defective.

### George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" (1946)

Primary contribution: resistance to stale phrasing, inflated diction, unnecessary words, and language that hides concrete meaning. The skill also adopts Orwell's higher-order idea that rules must yield when obeying them produces worse prose.

### Ernest Gowers, *Plain Words* (1948)

Primary contribution: plain language for consequential institutional writing, especially resistance to bureaucratic abstraction and needless formality.

### Richard Lanham, *Revising Prose* (1979 and later editions)

Primary contribution: revise mechanically enough to expose hidden actors, nominalizations, prepositional chains, and bureaucratic prose. This skill adopts the spirit of a separate structural revision pass before cosmetic editing.

### Joseph M. Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*

Primary contribution: readers understand prose more easily when grammatical subjects align with important characters and verbs express important actions; cohesion depends on how sentences move from familiar to new information.

### George Gopen and Judith Swan, "The Science of Scientific Writing" (1990)

Primary contribution: reader expectations around topic position, stress position, and information flow. This skill uses those ideas to choose sentence subjects from paragraph context rather than from a simplistic active-voice rule.

### Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*

Primary contribution: place the governing answer early and group supporting claims beneath it. This is especially useful for proposals, decisions, and leadership communication.

### ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

Primary contribution: disciplined technical instructions, consistent terminology, explicit actions, and reduced ambiguity. This skill borrows those concerns without claiming STE compliance or adopting its controlled vocabulary wholesale.

## Design stance

Treat the sources as competing lenses, not scripture. Several useful principles conflict:

- concision can remove evidence;
- plain vocabulary can erase technical precision;
- active voice can disrupt information flow;
- rigid sentence limits can damage natural explanation;
- executive compression can hide uncertainty.

Resolve conflicts by returning to the governing objective: reduce unnecessary reader effort while preserving accuracy, reasoning, and decision-relevant nuance.
